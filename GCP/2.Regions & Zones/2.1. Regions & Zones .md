# What are Regions?
- A Region is a geographic area that contains one or more data centers connected through a low-latency network.
Why Regions Exist

## Regions help organizations:
- Keep data closer to users
- Meet regulatory and compliance requirements
- Improve performance
- Enable disaster recovery
## Why Choosing the Right Region Matters
- Selecting the correct region impacts performance, cost, availability, and compliance.
- Latency and Performance : Choose a region closest to your users.
- Compliance and Data Residency : Many organizations must keep data within a specific country or geography.
- Service Availability : Not every cloud service is available in every region.
- Cost Optimization : Pricing can vary between regions.
- Business Continuity : Some regions offer better disaster recovery pairing options.
## Why Use Multiple Regions?
- Organizations often deploy applications in more than one region for reliability and global reach.
- Disaster Recovery (DR) : If one region experiences an outage, workloads can fail over to another region.
- High Availability : Applications remain accessible even if a regional issue occurs.
- Global User Base : Deploy applications closer to users worldwide.
- Load Distribution : Traffic can be spread across multiple regions.

# What are Availability Zones?
- Availability Zones (AZs) are physically separate datacenters (or groups of datacenters) within the same cloud region.
- Each Availability Zone has:
  - Independent power
  - Independent cooling
  - Independent networking
- This means if one zone experiences a failure, the other zones in the same region can continue operating.
- 
## Region vs Zone
- | Feature         | Region                                    | Availability Zone                         |
  | --------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
  | Geographic Area | Large geographic location                 | Datacenter location within a region       |
  | Distance        | Hundreds or thousands of kilometers apart | Usually within the same metropolitan area |
  | Latency         | Higher between regions                    | Very low between zones                    |
  | Purpose         | Disaster Recovery and Global Presence     | High Availability                         |
  | Example         | Central India and South India             | Zone 1, Zone 2, Zone 3 in Central India   |
